Anycubic Slicer MCP

An MCP server for Anycubic Slicer Next. Tell your AI agent what to print and it slices your STL, 3MF or STEP file, shows you the toolpaths and gives you the time and filament. Nothing prints until you say yes.

Or click your app to copy just its setup.

Previews

See every layer before the nozzle heats up.

The first layer

Check the first layer's walls and fill before anything else. Most failed prints start here. preview_gcode draws any layer coloured by feature.

Any layer, up close

Pick layer 12 or layer 40. Outer walls, inner walls, infill, bridges and supports each get their own colour, so a thin wall or a missing bridge is easy to spot.

The whole part at once

Every layer stacked and shaded by height. Lighter means higher. It's the quickest way to confirm the part sits where you expect on the bed.

From the side

A front elevation of the print, so overhangs and supports show up at a glance.

Watch it build

animate_layers saves a GIF of the print going up layer by layer. print_card makes a 1200×630 summary you can drop into a chat or an order.

Beyond slicing

The jobs that used to eat your evening.

Filament profiles, failed 3D prints, weak parts and wasted filament. Hand them to the agent and it does the fiddly part with the slicer you already use.

Hand it the whole project.

Point the agent at a folder or a .zip of parts. plan_project finds every model, reads counts from names like clip_x4.stl and from the README, lays each part in its best orientation and packs them onto plates. Group for the fewest plates or by colour, keep plates under a time limit, and keep parts that need supports together.

plan_projectslice_project

It asks before it guesses.

  • Two versions of the same partwhich one?
  • A part 1.6 mm wideinches?
  • Bigger than the bedscale or skip?
  • No count anywhereone of each?
  • Looks like a test pieceleave it out?

Nothing is sliced until you've answered the questions that matter and approved the plates.

Snap the spool label. Get a filament profile.

Send a photo of the label. The agent reads the brand, material, colour and temperature ranges, and create_filament_profile builds a preset from them, starting from the closest one your printer already has. You see a preview first and nothing is saved until you say so. Anything the label doesn't say gets a typical value for that material, and the agent tells you which ones.

The preview for a PETG label that reads 230 to 250 °C nozzle and 70 to 80 °C bed. Spools also go into a small inventory on your computer, so later the agent can tell you which one has enough left for a print.

Send it. Then keep an eye on it.

With LAN mode on, the agent talks to the printer directly: printer_status reads the state, temperatures, filament slots and any error code with Anycubic's meaning, printer_camera_snapshot looks at the bed, and send_print uploads and starts a file after checking the printer is idle and each filament matches its slot. Keep the printer on Anycubic Cloud instead and the agent goes through the slicer window you're signed in to: cloud_printer_status reads its Workbench and can look through its camera, and print_from_slicer uses its Start Print dialog. Starting, pausing and stopping always wait for your yes.

Show it the failed print.

Stringing, warping, split layers, a messy first layer. The agent looks at your photos and matches them to one of 13 known defects. diagnose_print then suggests setting changes based on the presets you actually used. When two fixes pull in opposite directions, it says so and leaves that setting alone.

diagnose_print

Make it stronger on purpose.

Say how the part gets loaded: pulled, pushed, bent, hit or twisted, and in which direction. analyze_strength finds thin waists in the shape, then suggests an orientation plus walls, infill and layer height. find_best_orientation ranks ways to lay it on the bed for less support, speed, strength or adhesion.

analyze_strengthfind_best_orientation

Less purge, more print.

Every colour change on a single-nozzle printer flushes filament. In a test on a Kobra X with two 15 mm cubes, one black and one white, 8.31 g of a 9.99 g print went to colour changes. optimize_multicolor measures that waste for your model, tries settings that might reduce it and recommends one only if every colour survives and it really saves filament. It also suggests layout changes, like fewer colour switches per layer.

Local and careful

Your printer stays yours.

Nothing prints without your yes.

Starting, pausing, resuming or stopping a print always comes as a preview first. The agent asks you, and the tool acts only once you say yes in the chat. Window buttons that print, stop or delete are locked the same way.

Runs on your machine.

It drives the slicer's own command line. Models, presets and spool records stay on your computer. The LAN tools only talk to addresses on your own network, and the printer's login stays in memory. It never logs in to Anycubic Cloud: cloud printing goes through the slicer window you're signed in to.

Refuses risky files.

Project files that carry post-processing scripts are rejected, since those scripts can run programs. Custom G-code templates are off limits unless you turn them on.

Questions.

    

      
Does it send anything to my printer?
Only when you say so. send_print (LAN mode) and print_from_slicer (Anycubic Cloud) start a print, and control_print pauses, resumes or stops one. Each shows you what it will do first and acts only after you say yes in the chat. Everything else only reads.

      
Can an AI agent slice STL files for my Anycubic printer?
Yes. Point your agent at an STL, 3MF, OBJ or STEP file and it runs Anycubic Slicer Next on your computer with the printer, process and filament you choose. You get G-code, a printer-ready .gcode.3mf, the print time, the filament weight and pictures of the toolpaths.

      
Which printers does it support?
Any printer that has a preset in Anycubic Slicer Next, because the server reads the slicer's own presets. That covers the Kobra X, Kobra S1, Kobra 4, Kobra 3, Kobra 2 and Kobra 1 families (see the full list). It has been tested end to end on a Kobra X with a 0.4 mm nozzle. If you run it on another model, a printer report helps a lot.

      
Which file formats does it accept?
STL, 3MF, OBJ and STEP models, or a whole folder or .zip of them for a multi-part project. It writes standard G-code and a .gcode.3mf you can send to the printer or open in the slicer.

      
Does it work with OrcaSlicer or Bambu Studio?
Not yet. It drives Anycubic Slicer Next, which is built on OrcaSlicer. Support for other OrcaSlicer-based slicers would be a welcome contribution.

      
Which AI apps can use it?
Anything that speaks the Model Context Protocol over stdio: Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Cursor, VS Code, Codex, Antigravity, DeepSeek Harness and others.

      
Do I need to keep the slicer window open?
Not for slicing. The server runs the slicer from the command line in the background, using the printer, process and filament you last picked in the app, so open it once to choose them. The Anycubic Cloud tools and the window tools do need it open.

      
Can my agent see what the printer is doing?
Yes, in either mode. With LAN mode on (on the printer: Settings > Network > LAN Mode), printer_status reports the state, temperatures, the filament in each slot, how the last print ended and any error code with Anycubic's meaning, and printer_camera_snapshot takes a photo with the built-in camera. With the printer on Anycubic Cloud, cloud_printer_status reads the same kind of thing from the slicer window's Workbench screen and can take a picture with its camera. A Kobra X can't do both: LAN mode switches off the cloud and the phone app's remote control.

      
Does it work with Anycubic Cloud and the phone app?
Yes, through the slicer window. Keep LAN mode off and stay signed in to Anycubic Slicer Next. cloud_printer_status reads the printer from the window's Workbench, and print_from_slicer starts the plate you sliced there through its Start Print dialog. The server never sees your Anycubic login. This works on macOS for now and needs Screen Recording and Accessibility permission for the app that runs the server.

      
Does it work on Linux?
Not yet. It supports Windows and macOS. Pull requests for Linux are welcome.

      
My slicer is installed somewhere unusual.
Set ANYCUBIC_SLICER_EXE to the slicer program and, if needed, ANYCUBIC_SLICER_DATA to its data folder, in your MCP client's config for this server.

      
Is this made by Anycubic?
No. It's an independent open-source project under the MIT licence and isn't affiliated with or endorsed by Anycubic.
